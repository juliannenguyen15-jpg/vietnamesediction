How to say endings, and the IPA symbols

Endings are stopped, not released. For -p, -t, -c, and -ch, close your lips or tongue and stop the sound without popping it out the way English does in “cat.” For -m, -n, -ng, and -nh, keep the sound nasal.

IPA used: ʌ as in “cup” (ơ, â) · ʊ for ư · ɛ for e · ŋ as “ng” in “sing” · ɲ like Spanish “ñ” (nh) · d̪ a soft, dental d (đ) · tɕ like “ch” (tr, ch) · z for r, d, gi (Northern) · j as in “yes” for d, gi, v (Southern) · ɹ like English “r” for r (Southern) · a small mark after a letter, as in k̚, means the ending is stopped.
